# frontier — a commons of what works

`reference-frontier.json` is where the field gets better together. It's not a
scoreboard; it's a living collection of validated leverage — the techniques that
turn tokens into durable work, each carried with the evidence that it worked. When
you run `dyno-tune`, this is what lets it say "setups like yours that tried this
saw that," pointing at something real someone actually did, not an opinion.

## The flywheel

You bring your wins to the commons, and you take home everyone else's. The moment
one setup finds a real unlock, the *technique* behind it lands here, and on
everyone's next run it's on the menu, matched to setups like theirs. Someone 10x's
a workflow; the pattern is waiting for the rest of us. Generosity is the growth
engine, and the frontier rises as anyone rises.

## Contributing (opt-in, anonymized)

Run `dyno-report` or `dyno-tune`, let it draft an entry, and open a PR. An entry
carries the part that *transfers*:

- the **technique** — the engine configuration that produced the result, described
  so someone else can adopt it,
- the **fingerprint** — engine class, model roles, effort, review regime,
- the **efficiency vector**, the survival **horizon**, and the sample size,
- a date.

It carries **nothing** that identifies you: no name, no repo names, no code, no
product information. The commons compares technique and results, never people, and
never against product outcomes — that's the governance line (`docs/governance.md`).
Bring only your own wins, and only with your own consent.

The seed entries are from one operator's Claude Code logs at the same-session
survival floor (Aug 2026) — a starting point, not a verdict. Better data,
especially at day/week horizons and from other harnesses, is exactly what we're
hoping you'll add.
